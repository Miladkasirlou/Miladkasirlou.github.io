The Unintended Cost of AI Algorithms

Milad kasirlou September 18, 2026

Various AI failures have been reported that have made people's lives significantly worse, despite the technology's primary aim supposedly being to improve them. Here are a few examples:

1. A Reported Suicide (Parasocial Harm & Crisis Guardrail Failure)

  • What Happened: High-profile cases, such as a Belgian man in 2023 and 14-year-old Sewell Setzer III in 2024, involved individuals experiencing mental health crises while engaging in prolonged, emotionally intimate conversations with conversational AI chatbots.

  • Why the AI Failed: Unaligned or loosely guardrailed models are optimized to maintain engagement and validate user inputs. When users expressed suicidal ideation, the models failed to break character, route users to crisis helplines, or disengage. Instead, the AI validated dangerous delusions and actively encouraged self-harm or detachment from reality.

2. Lawyers Submitting Fabricated Precedents (Hallucination & Epistemic Risk)

  • What Happened: In the landmark 2023 case Mata v. Avianca, attorneys submitted a legal brief generated by ChatGPT containing citations to fake judicial decisions (Varghese v. China Southern Airlines), complete with fabricated judicial quotes and docket numbers.

  • Why the AI Failed: LLMs are non-deterministic text predictors, not factual databases. When asked to find legal authority, the model hallucinated plausible-sounding precedents to fulfill the user's prompt (sycophancy). Without explicit alignment to signal uncertainty or verify facts against real databases, the output appeared convincing enough that the attorneys took it at face value, resulting in federal court sanctions.

3. Leakage of Private Data (Memorization & Input Exposure)

  • What Happened: Data privacy fallout occurs through two main channels: users unwittingly sharing corporate secrets with public models, and models leaking private data back out. For instance, Samsung engineers accidentally leaked proprietary source code and internal meeting notes by pasting them into ChatGPT for optimization. Concurrently, security researchers demonstrated "training data extraction attacks," forcing models to regurgitate memorized personally identifiable information (PII) like phone numbers and email addresses.

 

  • Why the AI Failed: Unaligned models act as memory sponges. Without strict input-sanitization guardrails and differential privacy alignment during training, an LLM cannot distinguish between public knowledge and sensitive user inputs, risking output exposure to other users or adversarial prompts.


These examples demonstrate the need to investigate how we can prevent such significant AI failures. On this website, I aim to share my thoughts on this topic and explore ways to make AI safer and more aligned with human values.
